\begin{abstract}
\noindent
This paper examines whether political disinformation is associated with
growth and whether banking stress amplifies that association. A frozen panel
of 175 countries over 2000--2025 combines expert-coded Digital Society Project
indicators with macroeconomic outcomes. The index describes the political
information environment rather than false financial messages. In the baseline
three-year regression, \GrowthTwoN{} observations from \GrowthTwoCountries{}
countries yield a cumulative-growth slope of $\GrowthTwo$ percentage points
per index standard deviation (standard error $\GrowthTwoSE$), evaluated at
mean conditioning states. The banking-stress interaction is $\FragilityTwo$
(standard error $\FragilityTwoSE$). Sample and conditioning choices affect
these estimates. Country-block bootstrap contrasts do not distinguish the
lower-tail slope from the median or upper tail. Adding the index worsens
tenth-quantile forecast loss by $\ForecastTailDeterioration$ percent in a
chronological evaluation; the gain interval includes zero. An illustrative
financing model explains how signal contamination and financial fragility
can interact, without implying unequal quantile slopes. The results separate
conditional associations, distributional differences and incremental
predictive value. They do not identify causal financial-message harm,
crisis thresholds or returns to information policy.
\end{abstract}
