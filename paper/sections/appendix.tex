\section{Proofs and Formalization Scope}\label{app:proofs}

\subsection{Scalar state dynamics}
The fixed-point equation $x=c+\rho x$ gives $\mu=c/(1-\rho)$.
Subtracting it from the recursion yields $x_{t+1}-\mu=\rho(x_t-\mu)$,
which proves the closed form and convergence for $|\rho|<1$.
Logistic bounds and strict monotonicity follow from $e^{-x}>0$.
With independent innovations, the stationary representation is
$x_t=\mu+\sum_{j\ge0}\rho^ju_{t-j}$ and the variance follows by a geometric
sum. Solving $\rho^h=1/2$ gives the latent half-life formula. These statements
condition on absent or fixed external inputs; they are not a stationarity
proof for the fitted regional panel.

For fixed nonnegative row-stochastic $W$, the vector of ones is an eigenvector
with eigenvalue one. Hence $\rho+\delta$ is an eigenvalue of
$A=\rho I+\delta W$. The induced infinity norm is $\rho+\delta$, so the
spectral radius is exactly that value for nonnegative $\rho,\delta$.
A time-varying network or country-dependent interaction coefficients require
a separate stability analysis.

For equation~\eqref{eq:index-deviation}, apply the mean-value theorem between
$\mu$ and $\mu+\rho^td_0$. The derivative bound $\sigma'\le1/4$ gives
the stated inequality; a first-order expansion at $\mu$ gives the local
approximation. Differentiating $h(\rho)$ yields
equation~\eqref{eq:half-sensitivity}, and
$-\log(\rho)/(1-\rho)\to1$ gives its limiting expression. These steps
explain both the bounded scale of index changes and the sensitivity of
half-life transformations without assuming that boundedness guarantees
stationarity of the latent process.

\subsection{Signal noise and the lending assumption}
Independence gives $\E[b\eta]=0$ and
$\mathrm{Var}(b\eta d)=d^2\E[b\eta^2]=pd^2$. Adding independent
$\varepsilon$ proves Lemma~\ref{lem:pollution}. The posterior distribution
of $V$ has not been specified; the lemma does not compute its variance.
Under the assumed credit rule~\eqref{eq:credit},
\[
 I^*-\min\{I^*,W_0+L(p)\}
 =\max\{\lambda\gamma d^2p+I^*-W_0-\lambda\bar V
                   +\lambda\gamma\sigma_\varepsilon^2,0\}.
\]
Collecting the financing-pressure terms gives~\eqref{eq:harm}. This is a
conditional derivation from a stated reduced-form lending rule, not a
Bayesian microfoundation.

\subsection{Increasing differences}
Let $g(z)=\max\{z,0\}$, $s=ap_1+bF_1-\kappa_0$,
$u=a(p_2-p_1)\ge0$ and $v=b(F_2-F_1)\ge0$.
The increment $g(s+u)-g(s)$ is nondecreasing in $s$, which implies
$g(s+u)-g(s)\le g(s+v+u)-g(s+v)$. This proves increasing differences.
When $ap_2+bF-\kappa_0\le0$, both terms of the contamination increment
are zero. No quantitative claim about mean or quantile growth effects follows
without further assumptions on aggregation and other shocks.

\subsection{Aggregation across financing thresholds}
To prove Proposition~\ref{prop:aggregation}, write $z=ap+bF$ and
\[
 A(z)=\E[(z-K)_+]=\int_{-\infty}^{z}(z-k)f_K(k)\,dk.
\]
The integrand is integrable under the finite-moment assumption. Its
difference quotient with respect to $z$ is bounded in absolute value by one
and converges to $\mathbf{1}\{K<z\}$ except at $K=z$, which has zero
probability. Dominated convergence gives $A'(z)=C_K(z)$; continuity of the
density then gives $A''(z)=f_K(z)$. The chain rule establishes
$\overline H_p=aA'(z)$ and $\overline H_{pF}=abA''(z)$. Applying it again
to $-\chi A(ap(m)+bF)$ proves equation~\eqref{eq:growth-cross}.
If the threshold distribution has atoms, the smooth derivative expression
can fail at those atoms, although the individual increasing-differences
inequality still survives averaging over a fixed distribution.

The condition that the distribution is fixed is consequential. For a
conditional distribution $C_K(k\mid p,F)$, changes in $p$ or $F$ alter
both individual shortfalls and population composition. Differentiating the
expectation then introduces distributional terms with unrestricted signs.
The proposition therefore identifies a particular constraint channel rather
than a universal restriction on observed country-level growth.

For equation~\eqref{eq:location-scale}, positive $s$ preserves the ordering
of the realizations of $\epsilon$. Quantile equivariance gives
$Q_\tau=\ell+s q_\tau$, so
$\partial_m Q_\tau-\partial_m Q_\nu=s_m(q_\tau-q_\nu)$.
If $s_m=0$, all such contrasts vanish while $\ell_{mF}$ can remain negative.
This provides an explicit example in which fragility amplification and equal
quantile slopes hold simultaneously.

\subsection{Feedback and multiplicity}
The map in~\eqref{eq:feedback} is globally Lipschitz with constant at most
$\rho+\varphi L_g$. When this bound is below one, Banach's fixed-point
theorem on the complete space $\R$ gives existence, uniqueness and global
convergence. In the piecewise-linear example, solving on each of
$x\le0$, $0\le x\le1$ and $x\ge1$ gives respectively $-3/2$, $1/2$ and
$5/2$. Slopes at those roots are $1/2$, $5/2$ and $1/2$.
The example proves that multiplicity is possible, not that it begins when
an upper bound on a derivative reaches one.

\subsection{Elasticities and the optional burden bound}
For $c>0$ and $0<\rho<1$,
\[
 \frac{\partial\mu}{\partial c}=\frac1{1-\rho},\qquad
 \frac{\partial\mu}{\partial\rho}=\frac{c}{(1-\rho)^2},\qquad
 \frac{\partial^2\mu}{\partial\rho^2}=\frac{2c}{(1-\rho)^3}.
\]
Multiplying the first derivatives by $c/\mu$ and $\rho/\mu$ establishes
Proposition~\ref{prop:burden}. The same algebraic elasticity identity extends
to $c\ne0$, but negative $c$ reverses the desired direction of a reduction
in persistence. At $c=0$, division by $\mu$ is undefined. Applying the chain
rule to expenditure gives~\eqref{eq:cost}.

The marginal comparison can also be distinguished from a finite parameter
change. Let $0<e<1$ denote an equal proportional reduction, holding the other
parameter fixed. The proportional declines in the positive latent steady
state are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-reduction}
 \frac{\mu-\mu_c(e)}{\mu}=e,\qquad
 \frac{\mu-\mu_\rho(e)}{\mu}
 =\frac{e\rho}{1-\rho+e\rho},
\end{equation}
where $\mu_c(e)=(1-e)c/(1-\rho)$ and
$\mu_\rho(e)=c/\{1-(1-e)\rho\}$. Since the denominators are positive,
the persistence reduction is larger precisely when $\rho>1/(2-e)$.
As $e\downarrow0$, this threshold tends to $1/2$, recovering the
infinitesimal comparison. For positive finite $e$ the threshold is higher.
Thus the local elasticity ranking is not a formula for arbitrarily large
interventions. Applying the increasing logistic function preserves the
ordering of the two deterministic endpoint changes, but not their
proportional magnitudes or their welfare interpretation.

For completeness, if $M_h\in[0,1]$, $|Y_h|\le\bar Y$ and $\kappa>0$,
then $D_H=\sum_{h=0}^H(1+\kappa)^{-h}M_hY_h$ satisfies
$|D_H|\le\bar Y(1+\kappa)/\kappa$ by the triangle inequality and the
geometric-series bound. Signed outcomes permit negative scores. This bound
is not a welfare result and does not validate that normalization.

\subsection{What the Lean files establish}
The repository includes proofs of logistic bounds and monotonicity;
scalar deterministic dynamics and a geometric variance identity;
increasing differences of the max-linear harm function; uniqueness given a
contraction bound and an explicit three-root example; an absolute burden
bound; and algebraic derivative, elasticity and midpoint-convexity results.
These are selected mathematical statements, not a machine verification of
all economic interpretations. The aggregation, location-scale and finite-change
derivations above are analytical extensions; they are not included in the
companion Lean formalizations. In particular, the files do not establish
construct validity, posterior-risk microfoundations, empirical identification,
forecast performance, intervention effectiveness, stochastic ergodicity of
the full panel, or a data-calibrated bifurcation threshold. The formalization
mapping and build instructions are recorded in the repository.

measurement process, time-varying confounding, the forecasting

\section{Synthesis of the Analytical and Empirical Results}\label{app:synthesis}
The proofs give a coherent interpretation of the evidence when the
information environment, financing constraints and growth distribution are
kept conceptually distinct. Persistence describes how an aggregate latent
index evolves. The constraint model describes how signal noise can restrict
investment, and its aggregation shows why the concentration of borrowers
near a financing boundary matters for amplification. Neither persistence nor
amplification alone determines the relative slopes of growth quantiles.

This distinction helps reconcile the numerical findings. The negative
baseline growth slope and banking-stress interaction are compatible with a
financing channel, subject to the proxy mapping and the sensitivity to sample
and conditioning choices. The quantile contrasts do not establish an
additional lower-tail concentration of the association. The forecast
comparison asks a further question---whether the index contributes useful
information beyond available macro-financial predictors---and finds no
improvement in the evaluated design. These results address different
properties of the data, so agreement on every dimension is not required for
a coherent interpretation.

The policy implications remain conditional on the same structure. Persistence
can magnify a positive latent steady state, but the sign of the intercept,
the size of the parameter change and the cost of changing it determine any
intervention ranking. A mathematical possibility of multiple steady states
does not locate an empirical crisis threshold. The combined analysis thus
provides a basis for testing financial-information exposure and financing
constraints more directly, while keeping the measured associations separate
from causal welfare calculations.
