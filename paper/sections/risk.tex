\section{What Can Be Quantified as Risk?}\label{sec:risk}

The defensible quantitative outputs are conditional growth profiles,
uncertainty intervals, observed banking-stress interactions, and held-out
forecast losses. At an observed stress value $f$, the reported profile is
$\hat\beta_h+\hat\phi_{F,h}f$, with other conditioning states at their
reference values. It should be evaluated within the empirical support and
with its covariance-based uncertainty. A change in this profile is not an
estimated intervention effect.

The horizon convention also matters for economic interpretation. The growth
outcome at $h=2$ adds three annual growth rates. A slope of $\GrowthTwo$
percentage points is therefore a difference in that sum, holding regressors
fixed; it is not an annual growth penalty to be applied three more times.
Compounded output growth instead depends on the product of annual gross
growth factors, and recovering it requires a path of annual rates. Moreover,
the horizon-specific regressions can use different samples, so differences
between their cumulative slopes need not recover a common annual profile.

Projecting a sustained change in the index into a structural growth scenario
would require explicit dynamic equations,
shock identification, and validation of the mapping from interventions to
parameters. Those ingredients are not supplied by the current panel.

Combining growth, inequality and banking outcomes into a single burden score
requires economically meaningful units and weights. Normalizing a channel by
its estimated two-year response is unstable when that response is near zero
and uncertain. Allowing signed outcomes can also reverse a ranking based on
banking stability alone. A bounded mathematical score does not establish
welfare meaning, comparability, or reliable policy rankings. The appendix
retains the elementary boundedness result solely to document the mathematical
scope of this construction. No GDP-loss totals, burden reductions,
or empirical trap thresholds are inferred from it.
